How the data works

Reported delays, with their limits in view.

Every five minutes

We poll selected stations using Iarnród Éireann's public realtime API every 5 minutes. Pages refresh every 60 seconds. All displayed times use Europe/Dublin, including daylight saving time.

What “late” means

The API's Late field reports delay in minutes. “On time” in the headline means 0–1 minutes late. Scheduled times are timetable times, using the arrival time for terminating trains.

A last reading, not a confirmed arrival

Each new poll replaces the stored reading for that train at that station on that date. The retained value is the last reading before a train leaves the board, not a confirmed arrival delay. A reading can change while the train remains on the board.

Network summaries count each train once using its latest station reading. Details show the train code and available earlier readings at other stations; they are not a complete poll history or a live train location.

Coverage and gaps

A configured station is reporting when it has any reading in the last 30 minutes. Limited coverage means fewer than all configured stations are reporting. Stations without a recent reading are labelled “No recent readings”.

We do not store empty successful fetches or fetch failures. A gap could reflect no trains on the board, a connection problem, or missing API data; we cannot distinguish these from the stored readings. A network figure describes tracked trains, not the whole railway.

Starts, calls and terminations

“Terminates here” means the destination matches the station name; “Starts here” means the origin matches. Otherwise we show “Calling service”. These labels are name-based inferences, so differences in names can affect them.

Independent project

Built and maintained by Daniel English. This is an independent project, not affiliated with Iarnród Éireann, using their public realtime API.
